Icon of the New Testament Trinity

Christ, the Father as the Ancient of Days, and the Holy Spirit

Tempera and gold on poplar panel, 35.5 × 62.5 cm

Depicts
Holy Trinity, Jesus Christ, Pentecost, St Kosmas the Hymnographer, St Joseph the Hymnographer
Commemorated
Holy Pentecost: the Descent of the Holy Spirit (movable)
Monday of the Holy Spirit (movable)
St Kosmas the Hymnographer, Bishop of Maiouma, October 14
April 4
Period
c. 1450
School

Kept at
Cleveland Museum of ArtCleveland

Christ sits on the left of a great gilded throne. Beside him is the Ancient of Days from the vision of Daniel, God the Father shown as Christ in old age. Between them the dove of the Holy Spirit hovers in an eight-pointed star, the sign of the eighth day, the age to come. This 'New Testament Trinity' became common in late Byzantine art. Rublev's Hospitality of Abraham, also in this library, approaches the mystery quite differently.

At the sides, two hymnographers lean from arched windows with their scrolls: St Kosmas of Maiouma on the left and St Joseph the Hymnographer on the right. The icon probably stood on a church templon. The panel was painted in Constantinople around 1450, only a few years before the city fell to the Ottomans in 1453. That makes it one of the last icons of the imperial capital.
